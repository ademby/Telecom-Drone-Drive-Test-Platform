# The Drone Drive-Test Platform

The platform plans and executes telecom drone drive-test missions, receives their measurements, validates results, and presents approved geographic coverage data to telecom operators. It spans operator workflows, backend orchestration, and drone integration.

Terms below define the ubiquitous language. Where the current implementation is narrower than the concept, a short **Today** note says so. Architecture is described in [`architecture/frontend.md`](architecture/frontend.md) and [`architecture/backend.md`](architecture/backend.md).

## Platform

**Operator**:
The human who plans missions, monitors execution, validates uploaded results, and explores approved measurements.
_Today_: there is no operator entity, account or authentication; the operator is whoever uses the UI.

**Drone**:
A managed execution device with its own identity, capabilities, status, and authenticated relationship to the platform.
_Today_: a drone is only a `droneId` string on a mission (the editor offers two hard-coded ids). There is no drone entity, registry, status or authentication; the backend checks that the `droneId` in a claim or status report equals the mission's assigned drone.

**Operational area**:
A geographic scope in which missions and approved measurements are managed. It may contain one or more countries and administrative hierarchies.
_Today_: a concept only. It is not modeled, stored or required by any mission (ADR-0008).

**Administrative geography**:
Versioned reference regions used for navigation, filtering, constraints, and reporting. A mission route is not owned by an administrative region.
_Today_: a preprocessed GeoJSON hierarchy (ADM0 root, `adminLevel`, `parentId`) generated by `tools/data-pipeline/admin-boundaries` and loaded by the frontend for navigation and location search only. The backend does not know about it, and it is not versioned in the platform.

## Mission execution

**Mission**:
An operator-defined, route-centric drive-test plan that may accumulate execution history, progressing through draft, planned, dispatched, running, completed, failed, or cancelled states. A mission owns immutable route revisions. The mission name may provide a human-readable description of the covered region; a dedicated descriptive region field or metadata may be introduced later if the domain requires it.

**Route revision**:
An immutable geographic version of a mission route (a GeoJSON WGS84 `LineString` with at least two coordinates). Editing a draft's route creates a new revision. An execution references one specific route revision, preserving the exact route used for that execution.

**Execution**:
The act of executing a mission. Execution is currently implicit rather than represented by a first-class `ExecutionAttempt` domain entity. A failed mission can produce a new derived mission; it is not silently retried in place. A derived mission is a new draft that copies name, drone, schedule and the active route and records `derivedFrom`.

**Dispatch**:
The point at which a planned mission has been atomically claimed by its assigned drone and is eligible for execution.

**Missed dispatch**:
A terminal mission failure (`MISSED_DISPATCH`) caused by the dispatch deadline passing before a drone claims the mission. A backend sweeper detects it periodically (every 10 seconds).

**Failure reason**:
Why a mission is `FAILED`: `MISSED_DISPATCH` (set by the platform), or `EXECUTION_FAILED` / `DATA_INVALID` (reported by the drone).

**Cancellation**:
An operator command that ends any non-terminal mission (`DRAFT`, `PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED`, `RUNNING`) as `CANCELLED`.

**Idempotent command**:
A mutating mission or result command that carries an `Idempotency-Key`. Repeating a key returns the current state of the mission instead of applying the command again.

## Measurements and results

**Measurement**:
An immutable observation collected during mission execution at a specific point in space and time. It contains collected/raw network observations (`rawObservations`, for example `signalQuality`) and validity information; derived KPI values are not part of the measurement itself.

**Mission result**:
The complete dataset uploaded for one mission execution, including its measurements and upload identity (`deviceId`, `uploadedAt`). A mission has at most one result, and a result can only be uploaded for a `COMPLETED` mission.

**Result revision**:
An immutable validation view of a mission result that records which measurements are **rejected** (acceptance is implicit: a measurement not rejected is accepted) and, once finalized, when it was finalized. A correction is represented by a new revision, never by mutating an existing one.

**Approved measurement**:
A measurement included by the active finalized result revision (that is, not rejected by it) and therefore eligible for approved projections.

**Projection**:
A reproducible derived view of approved measurements, such as a mission summary, administrative aggregation, or continuous geographic surface. A projection is available for KPI exploration only after the corresponding mission result has been finalized.
_Today_: only the global Signal Quality surface exists. Mission summaries and administrative aggregations are not implemented.

## Visualization

**Global surface**:
A projection of approved measurements across an operational area for a selected scope and time interval.
_Today_: it covers all approved measurements of all missions; scope and time-interval selection are not implemented.

**Visualization policy**:
The interpretation metadata for a projection, including KPI units/range, no-data behavior, and presentation defaults.
_Today_: the range (`min`, `max`, `version`) comes from the backend; no-data is `NaN` (transparent); palette, opacity and thresholds are frontend defaults.

**Projection version**:
An opaque string that changes whenever the approved set changes. Clients use it to cache-bust numeric tiles.

**Data tile**:
A spatially bounded numeric tile used to render a projection: a 64 x 64 row-major grid of `Float32` values (`NaN` = no data) addressed by Web Mercator `z/x/y`. It contains data values, not presentation colors.

**Validation**:
The operator's review of a mission result, including reversible acceptance/rejection decisions (held locally until saved) and finalization of a result revision.

## Terminology

**KPI**:
A generic architectural/domain term for a key performance indicator derived from measurement data. It is not the name of a specific feature.

**Signal Quality**:
The concrete KPI currently exposed by the platform. `SignalQuality` should therefore be used for the specific feature and its domain behavior, while `KPI` is reserved for generic abstractions or concepts shared by multiple indicators.

**KPI projection**:
A geographic or otherwise derived representation of a specific KPI over approved measurements. The current Signal Quality projection is only available after result finalization.

## Frontend vocabulary

These terms describe the operator application structure (ADR-0005, ADR-0009, ADR-0010).

**Workflow**:
A self-contained operator capability that owns its map layers, map interactions and controls, panel sections, and (if it talks to the backend) its HTTP adapters. There are three: navigation, mission, heatmap. Workflows never call each other.

**Composition root**:
The single place that builds the shared map surface and panel and constructs the workflows in dependency order.

**Map controller**:
The shared map surface: the OpenLayers map, basemap switching and view-animation helpers. It contains no feature layers.

**Operations panel**:
The floating panel that hosts workflow-provided views. It knows only the panel view contract.

**Panel view**:
A section a workflow contributes to the operations panel, optionally with a home-screen card.

**Renderer**:
The component that turns numeric tiles plus a palette into a map layer. Canvas (default) and WebGL implementations exist behind one interface.

**Palette**:
The client-side color ramp mapping normalized values to colors. Never sent to the backend.

**Advisory**:
A fading message on the map. After a result is finalized the operator sees an advisory to refresh the heatmap, because workflows do not notify each other (ADR-0011).

## Current operational decisions

- Missions are **route-centric**. An operational area is not currently required as a mission aggregate.
- The backend remains the **control authority / cockpit** for mission execution. Some state transitions require drone participation (claim, status reports), but the drone does not independently control the mission lifecycle.
- Drones **initiate** communication over REST (claim, status, upload); the backend does not call drones (ADR-0003).
- Result validation is currently **operator-driven**. The design should leave room for selectable validation policies later without making automated validation a current requirement.
- KPI exploration is based on **finalized results only**.
- Measurements represent **raw observations**; KPIs are derived after result finalization.
- Result revisions are **immutable**; corrections use a new revision. A finalized result may be revised again.
- The frontend is organized as **independent workflows** sharing only the map controller and the operations panel; cross-workflow change notification is not a direct call (interim: operator advisory; planned: SSE).

## Open questions

1. **Which revision is authoritative?** The intended rule is "exactly one _finalized_ revision is the current authoritative revision used for KPI projections". The implementation makes the _latest saved_ revision active even when it is not finalized, so saving a draft revision after a finalized one removes the mission from approved projections until it is finalized again. Decide whether to change the rule (latest revision always wins) or the code (only finalized revisions can become active, drafts tracked separately).
2. **Drone identity and authentication**: required before a real network (ADR-0003, `future-deployment.md` D-08).
3. **Drone registry**: the editor's drone list is hard-coded.
4. **Automated validation policies** and **operational area as an aggregate** remain open product decisions (`future-features.md`).
